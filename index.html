THE PROBLEM

Personal tax is still hard to navigate.

Taxpayers in Sri Lanka must interpret financial transactions, apply the correct year-specific legislation, understand legal provisions, and identify strategies suited to their circumstances. Online calculators rarely explain how a result was produced — or whether a recommendation fits the individual.

Open group thesis draft ↗

01

Fragmented records

Bank statements often contain short or ambiguous descriptions, making income categories hard to classify before tax processing begins.

02

Changing legislation

Amendments revise rates, reliefs, and eligibility. Calculations must stay tied to the correct year of assessment with reviewable references.

03

Opaque guidance

Relevant rules are spread across the Act and amendments. Non-specialists need answers that are understandable and evidence-supported.

04

Weak personalisation

A strategy that suits one taxpayer may not suit another. Recommendations need eligibility, impact estimates, and clear limitations.

FOUR MODULES

Four components.
One decision flow.

Each component owns a clear research subproblem, while sharing a common architecture that separates legally defined calculations from model-generated predictions.

A
IT22118936SAMARASINGHE K.A.E.H.N.

How should this transaction be read?

Component A interprets and categorises financial transactions so later tax processing can work from structured, consistent inputs rather than raw statement text.

  • Text processing and retrieval foundations for short or ambiguous transaction descriptions
  • Structured categories for employment, business, investment, transfers, and related activity
  • Clear boundary: classification supports analysis — it is not a final legal determination
  • Ambiguous cases are treated carefully and may require additional context or review
NLPCLASSIFICATIONINFORMATION RETRIEVAL
B
IT22064486CHALANIKA A.L.V.

Which rules apply this year — and why?

Component B applies assessment-year-specific tax rules, performs deterministic calculations, and presents explanations supported by relevant legal sources.

  • Year-aware rule application instead of one static calculator assumption
  • Deterministic tax computation grounded in defined rates, reliefs, and eligibility
  • Explanations and references that make results reviewable against legislation
  • Language-model or ML outputs are never treated as authoritative tax calculations
RULE ENGINEYAML RULESEXPLAINABILITY
C
IT22896186HEWAGAMA S.R.

Can tax guidance be clear and grounded?

Component C provides a natural-language advisory interface that retrieves relevant tax information and uses it to support responses to user questions.

  • Transformer-based language interaction for tax-related questions
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to connect answers with supporting sources
  • Checks for unsupported or incomplete responses in a regulatory context
  • Designed to assist understanding — not replace official determinations or advice
RAGNLPTRACEABLE ANSWERS
D
IT22238580PIHILLEGEDARA S.N.M.

Which strategy fits this taxpayer?

Component D ranks potentially suitable tax strategies for a taxpayer profile and estimates possible financial impacts under defined assumptions.

  • Personalised ranking informed by financial circumstances and preferences
  • Predictive modelling (e.g. gradient boosting) for impact estimation
  • Explainability support so users can inspect influential factors
  • Predictions presented as estimates with assumptions — not guaranteed outcomes

INTEGRATED METHOD

From records to reviewable advice — on one stack.

The integrated solution combines legislation analysis, rule-based processing, AI/information retrieval, machine-learning prediction, and explainability inside a shared application architecture (React · TypeScript · Vite · FastAPI).
